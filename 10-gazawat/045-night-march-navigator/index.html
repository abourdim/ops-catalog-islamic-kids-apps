❓ Help
What is Night March?

A canvas navigation puzzle where you guide a Muslim army through the night using stars, constellations, and the moon for navigation. Learn how historical Muslim commanders used celestial navigation.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once loaded, everything runs in your browser with no internet needed.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. Works on any device with a modern browser. Tap stars and paths on touch screens.

How do I change the theme?

Click any theme button in the controls bar. Midnight theme is default for the night sky feel.

How do I change the language?

Click EN / FR / ع. Arabic enables right-to-left layout automatically.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs locally in your browser. No data is sent anywhere.

🎯 Purpose

Learn celestial navigation as used by Muslim armies. Identify Polaris, constellations, and use the moon for timing.

☆ Polaris (North Star)

The brightest star near the north celestial pole. Muslim navigators used it extensively for both land and sea travel. It appears almost stationary while other stars rotate around it.

☾ Moon Timing

The moon's position indicates rough time. A crescent moon sets early; a full moon is visible all night. Commanders chose moonless nights for stealth.

⚔ Historical Night Marches

Khalid ibn al-Walid used night marches at Mu'tah (629 CE) to reposition forces. Sultan Mehmed II moved troops by night before the fall of Constantinople (1453 CE). Night movement gave tactical surprise.

🌈 Constellations

Ursa Minor (Little Bear) contains Polaris. Ursa Major (Big Dipper) points to Polaris. Orion indicates south. Muslim astronomers catalogued these extensively.
