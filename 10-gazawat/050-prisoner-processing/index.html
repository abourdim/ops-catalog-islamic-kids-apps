Purpose

Teach children the Islamic rules for treating prisoners of war with dignity, based on the Battle of Badr (2 AH / 624 CE).

Historical Context

After the Battle of Badr, the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) captured about 70 prisoners. He ordered the Companions to treat them well. Prisoners were fed the same food the Muslims ate. The Prophet consulted Abu Bakr and Umar about their fate and chose ransom. Literate prisoners who could not afford ransom were given a unique option: teach 10 Muslim children to read and write, and earn their freedom.

Key Principles

1) Provide food, water, and shelter. 2) Register names and tribes. 3) No torture or humiliation. 4) Offer Islam without compulsion. 5) Ransom or exchange. 6) Education as ransom (Badr's unique precedent). 7) Protect the elderly, sick, and wounded. 8) Keep families together when possible.

Lesson

Islam valued education so highly that it was accepted as ransom equal to money. The dignity of prisoners was established 1400 years before modern Geneva Conventions.
