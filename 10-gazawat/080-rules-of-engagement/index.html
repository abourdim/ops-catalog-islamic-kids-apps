What is Rules of Engagement?

A scenario-based ethics game about Islamic rules of war. You face moral dilemmas and must choose the response that aligns with Islamic principles of justice, mercy, and ethics.

Are these real Islamic rulings?

Yes. All scenarios are based on authentic teachings of the Prophet (peace be upon him) and instructions given by Abu Bakr al-Siddiq to Muslim armies, which form the foundation of Islamic law of war.

Why is this important?

Islam established rules of warfare centuries before modern conventions. The Prophet (peace be upon him) forbade killing civilians, destroying crops, harming animals, and mistreating prisoners — 1400 years before the Geneva Conventions.

Abu Bakr's Ten Rules

When sending armies, Abu Bakr al-Siddiq gave 10 commandments: Do not kill women, children, or the elderly. Do not cut trees. Do not destroy buildings. Do not harm monks in monasteries. Do not kill animals except for food. Do not burn crops. Do not be treacherous. Do not mutilate. Do not be cowardly. Do not harm those who surrender.

Protection of Civilians

The Prophet (peace be upon him) strictly forbade harming non-combatants. When he saw a woman killed in battle, he was deeply angered and said this was forbidden.

Treatment of Prisoners

The Quran commands feeding prisoners even when you yourself are in need (76:8). After Badr, the Prophet treated prisoners with such kindness that many accepted Islam.
