What is Battle of Yarmouk?

A canvas strategy game where you command the Muslim army during the decisive Battle of Yarmouk (636 CE) that opened the Levant to Islam.

How do I play?

Each day presents a tactical challenge. Click on the wing under attack to send Khalid's mobile reserve. On the final day, execute the decisive flanking maneuver.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once loaded, everything runs in your browser with no internet needed.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. Works on any device with a modern browser. Tap the canvas to interact.

How do I change the theme?

Click any theme button in the controls bar.

How do I change the language?

Click EN / FR / ع. Arabic enables right-to-left layout automatically.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs locally. No data is sent anywhere.

Historical Context

The Battle of Yarmouk (636 CE) was fought between the Muslim Arab forces and the Byzantine Empire near the Yarmouk River (modern Jordan/Syria border). It was one of the most decisive battles in history.

Khalid ibn al-Walid

The legendary Muslim commander who unified four separate armies under one command. His mobile reserve cavalry tactic was revolutionary — keeping a strong force to reinforce any threatened sector.

The Mobile Reserve

Khalid's key innovation: a fast cavalry force held behind the lines, ready to rush to any threatened wing. This concept is still studied in military academies today.

Women of Yarmouk

Muslim women like Hind bint Utba and Asma bint Abu Bakr played a crucial role. When some soldiers retreated, the women rallied them back to fight, showing extraordinary courage.

The Decisive Day

On the final day, Khalid led his cavalry behind the Byzantine army, cutting off their retreat to the bridge over the Yarmouk gorge. Trapped between the Muslim army and the deep ravine, the Byzantine army was decisively defeated.

Unified Command

Before Yarmouk, the Muslim forces operated as 4 separate armies under Abu Ubayda, Amr ibn al-As, Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, and Shurahbil. Khalid unified them into one coordinated force — a key lesson in leadership.
