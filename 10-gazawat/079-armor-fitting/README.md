# Armor Fitting

Interactive drag-and-drop game where kids equip a warrior with historical Islamic armor pieces, placing each item on the correct body position.

## Concept

The Prophet (peace be upon him) wore two coats of chainmail at the Battle of Uhud. The Quran itself references armor — Allah taught Prophet David to make coats of mail (Surah Saba, 34:10-11). Players learn about historical Islamic armor by fitting a warrior with authentic equipment.

## Gameplay

1. Click an armor piece from the tray to select it
2. Click the correct body zone on the warrior to place it
3. Correct placements glow green, wrong ones flash red
4. Info panel shows historical details about each piece
5. Fit all 7 pieces as fast as possible
6. Time is your score — faster is better (lower = better)

## Armor Pieces

| Piece | Zone | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| Bayda (Helmet) | Head | Steel helmet with nasal guard and chainmail neck protector |
| Dir' (Chainmail) | Torso | Interlocking iron rings — mentioned in Quran |
| Daraqa (Shield) | Left Arm | Round leather or metal shield |
| Sayf (Sword) | Right Arm | Curved or straight blade |
| Gauntlets | Hands | Armored gloves |
| Greaves | Legs | Metal shin and calf plates |
| Khuff (Boots) | Feet | Leather riding boots |

## Features

- Single HTML file, zero dependencies, fully offline
- 8 CSS themes: Terminal, Midnight, Ember, Arctic, Phosphor, Signal, Redshift, Sand
- Trilingual: English, French, Arabic (with RTL support)
- Splash screen with Bismillah
- Sidebar help panel with FAQ, How-To, and Wiki tabs
- Web Audio API sound effects (no audio files)
- Click-to-select, click-to-place interaction
- Visual body zones with position feedback
- Info panel with historical armor descriptions
- Progress bar tracking completion
- Best time persistence via localStorage (lower is better)

## Lesson

Preparation and protection: Islam teaches that taking precautions is wise, not a lack of trust in Allah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) set the example by wearing armor in battle. Understanding historical equipment helps children appreciate the practical wisdom of early Muslims.
