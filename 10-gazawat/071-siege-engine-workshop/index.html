What is Siege Engine Workshop?

An interactive game where you build historical siege engines used in Islamic military campaigns. Learn about catapults, trebuchets, and battering rams by assembling their components.

Is this historically accurate?

Yes. Siege engines like the manjaniq (catapult/trebuchet) were used in battles like the Siege of Ta'if (8 AH). The Prophet (peace be upon him) authorized their use.

How do I play?

Choose a siege engine type, then drag parts from the tray into the correct build slots. Once assembled, test it against a wall to see your power score.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once loaded, everything runs in your browser with no internet needed.

How do I change the theme?

Click any theme button in the controls bar.

Manjaniq (Catapult)

The manjaniq was a torsion or counterweight catapult used in Islamic warfare. It was notably used during the Siege of Ta'if in 8 AH when the Prophet (peace be upon him) besieged the Thaqif tribe.

Trebuchet

A larger, more powerful counterweight siege engine. Muslim engineers refined trebuchet designs, creating some of the most powerful siege engines of the medieval world.

Battering Ram (Dabbabah)

Called dabbabah in Arabic, it was a covered ram used to break gates. It protected soldiers inside from arrows and hot oil while they struck fortress doors.

Siege of Ta'if

After the Conquest of Mecca (8 AH), some tribes resisted. At Ta'if, Muslims used siege engines for the first time, showing the Prophet's adaptability in military strategy.
